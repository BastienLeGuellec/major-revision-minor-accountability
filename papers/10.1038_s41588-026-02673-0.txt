TITLE: Paired mutation calling and spatial transcriptomics identify cellular neighborhoods associated with the neoplastic outcome of mouse colitis

ABSTRACT:
AbstractIn the progression from inflammatory bowel disease to associated cancer, the clonal mutational landscape shifts from selection of mutations in inflammatory genes to selection for cancer-driver mutations. How prevalence and expansion of either type of mutant clones could be impacted by the cellular environments in which they arise and how this affects the neoplastic outcome of colitis remains unknown. Here we combine in vivo lineage tracing, in silico modeling, mutational profiling and spatial transcriptomics in a mouse model of colitis-associated tumorigenesis to capture clone fates associated with chronic inflammation. We identify epithelial- and immune-enriched neighborhoods and propose a model in which establishment of a reparative tissue environment facilitates tumor initiation by promoting the selection and expansion of pro-oncogenic clones, reducing the span of inflammation-resistant neighborhoods containing nononcogenic clones.

BODY:
MainInflammatory bowel disease (IBD) affects 7 million people worldwide and is associated with many co-morbidities, including the development of colitis-associated cancer (CAC)1. Previous studies have shown that, during the progression of IBD, mutations affecting genes implicated in mounting an inflammatory response are under positive selection in the colonic epithelium2, 3–4. In these studies, clones bearing such mutations exhibited an increased capacity to survive and expand in colonic tissue, which was linked with resistance to inflammatory insult. Mechanisms thought to underlie this resistance are diverse: mutations in the IL17 and NF-κB pathways act directly by dampening inflammatory signaling, while others such as Arid1a inactivation act through chromatin remodeling to alter the transcriptional response to inflammatory cues5. Extensive characterization of the mutational landscape of CAC has revealed a reduced prevalence of inflammation-resistance mutations in tumors arising in the context of IBD6, 7, 8, 9–10, hinting at a protective role against cancer. This could explain why most patients are only diagnosed with colorectal neoplasia more than a decade after their initial IBD diagnosis11,12. How selection of inflammation-resistant clones is influenced by the environment in which they arise and how this affects the balance between IBD pathogenesis and neoplastic transformation has not yet been defined, in part due to a lack of model systems.Using lineage tracing to profile clone dynamics in the well-characterized Muc2KO model of colitis13, 14–15, we observe discrete areas of the tissue in which clonal expansion is favored. In silico modeling of clone dynamics and inference of effective crypt fission rate defines the tissue repair process. The expansion of mutated clones is studied using targeted amplicon sequencing after chemical mutagen treatment of Muc2KO mice. Importantly, clones bearing mutations found in human IBD and associated cancer are identified. Combination with spatial transcriptomics data originating from the same samples allows pairwise identification and phenotypic characterization of cellular neighborhoods enriched for clones with pro-oncogenic or anti-inflammatory mutations, respectively, positively or negatively associated with the neoplastic outcome of chronic inflammation.ResultsLineage tracing captures increased clonal expansion in colitisDepletion of the intestinal mucus barrier due to constitutive knockout (KO) of both alleles of the Muc2 gene leads to chronic inflammation restricted to the mouse colon16 (Extended Data Fig. 1a,b). A discrete two-dimensional lattice with a hexagonal arrangement of sites and periodic boundary conditions representing a cylindrical geometry in the tangential direction was randomly populated with 80% unlabeled and 20% labeled crypts and evolved forward in time in a sequence of steps allowing the transition of lattice sites between three possible states: either empty or occupied by an unlabeled or labeled crypt (Fig. 1d). In this model, transitions between states at each lattice site are governed by the probability of clone fixation, crypt replication by fission and crypt fusion, based on the identities of the six neighboring sites20,21 (Methods and Supplementary Fig. 1). To replicate the effects of acute damage, the entire right side of the lattice was emptied and sites from the undamaged side allowed to repopulate the damaged side of the lattice.Before damage, the number of empty sites is maintained at a plateau due to a balancing of fission and fusion events involving labeled and unlabeled crypts, considered here as homeostasis (Fig. 1e). Increased aggregation was seen in the right (damaged) side of the lattice postdamage and persists at an elevated level compared with the left (undamaged) side even after a return to homeostasis (Fig. 1e,f), illustrating how the process of repair following damage drives a neutral expansion of clones in proximity to injury.Tissue repair favors fission-biased clonesMutations in cancer-driving genes such as Kras have been shown to confer a large fission bias to mutant clones22. This led to faster repair of the damaged right side (T = 80,000 simulation steps with fission advantage versus 115,315 without) (Fig. 1g,h), with fission-biased crypts consistently outcompeting wild-type crypts in both right and left sides over time. These findings underscore the translational relevance of this study.DiscussionMutant clones undergoing positive selection in IBD may impact pathogenesis and development of CAC. Detailed cataloging of mutations and assessment of their location and function in patients is complicated by the paucity of mutational events, limited sampling methods and confounding effects of treatment. Here, a mouse model of colitis was leveraged to profile inflammation and cancer associated mutant clones, and the selective environments in which they arise, which together shape the conditions favoring different phenotypic outcomes, including neoplasia. Enrichment for Mmp7 and other extracellular matrix remodeling genes in tumor neighborhoods (Supplementary Table 1), a signature of nascency49, supports the claim that this model captures early stages of tumor initiation, rarely accessible in human samples.Tissue repair and the associated extracellular matrix remodeling is commonly viewed as cancer promoting50,51. Recently, epigenetic memory of the repair state has been shown to accelerate tumor growth following oncogenic mutation52.Here, we outlined the process by which epithelial repair promotes clonal expansion by crypt fission and showed that mutations conferring higher fission capacity expand even more within the repairing epithelium. Using spatial transcriptomics, we linked the expansion of a cellular neighborhood associated with epithelial repair to increasing tumor burden and neutrophil-rich neighborhoods. Neutrophil infiltration is known to contribute to oxidative stress and genomic instability53. It is therefore plausible that recruitment of neutrophils promotes the malignant transformation of cells participating in epithelial repair.In contrast to tissue repair, we demonstrated that the establishment of an immune-refractory environment is negatively associated with tumor burden. This immune-refractory cellular neighborhood is positively associated with accumulation of mutant clones previously found to undergo positive selection in the nondysplastic epithelium of patients with IBD, including those harboring Arid1a inactivation or mutations in the IL-17 and NF-κB pathways2, 3–4. Expansion of these nontumor-associated mutated clones reported both in the present study and previous work suggests they may contribute to the maintenance of such immune-refractory environments. This aligns with recently published evidence that Arid1a-deficient crypt patches create immune-exclusion environments in the normal human colonic epithelium5.Despite evidence from previous studies indicating that the extent of these mutant clone expansions may increase the severity of ulcerative colitis2, 3–4, our work implies that establishment of an immune-refractory environment may simultaneously act to prevent expansion of tumor-associated mutations, thereby delaying malignant transformation in diseased patients. This is analogous to the expansion of Notch1 mutated clones in the esophagus, which could outcompete cancer-driver clones, limiting their ability for malignant transformation54,55.Our findings highlight the importance of strategies for long-term management of IBD that promote epithelial repair while limiting malignant transformation. This may be particularly relevant considering the incidence of IBD in children and trend toward younger age of onset56. Samples were sequenced in two separate runs by the CRUK Cambridge Institute Genomics Core on Illumina NovaSeqX Plus for 150-bp paired end reads.Mutation callingRePlow62 was used for mutation calling based on dual replicate amplicon coverage using combinatorial pooling of amplicons and applied to the first 20 million reads of each sample (Supplementary Table 7). In brief, RePlow exploits replicate library preparations to separate the contribution of background errors occurring in library preparation from sequencing errors occurring in sequencing. This is done with a statistical model that calculates the total log ratio of probabilities for variant candidates compared with background errors across all replicates simultaneously. In doing so, adjusted VAFs are constructed by subtracting the contribution from sequencing errors, whereas background error profiles for the error model are calculated independently for the six base pair substitution types (A > C, A > G, A > T, C > A, C > G, C > T) across the targeted regions. An additional stringent filtering step was applied whereby only variants with a positive log ratio of probabilities in both replicates individually were retained, to exclude false positive calls at low VAF values. Orthogonal validation of mutation calls was done using the Ampliconseq pipeline (https://github.com/crukci-bioinformatics/ampliconseq). In brief, variants are called using Vardict from sequence reads aligned to the reference genome (GRCm39). The pipeline models the background substitution noise at each amplicon position to identify and filter SNV calls with an allele fraction indistinguishable from noise. A minimum of 0.01% VAF was set as the lower detection threshold.
