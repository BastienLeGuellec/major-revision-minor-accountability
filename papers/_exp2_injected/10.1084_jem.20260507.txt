TITLE: A genetically regulated SKAP2 switch modulates human T lymphocyte adhesion and migration

ABSTRACT:
Spinella et al. linked a Crohn’s disease risk allele regulating SKAP2 expression to T lymphocyte function. SKAP2 enhances cell adhesion while suppressing migration. Sustained TCR signaling triggers its proteolytic cleavage to enable migration. Overall, genetic alteration of SKAP2 levels drives T cell dysfunction and promotes autoimmune disease.

BODY:
IntroductionBeyond their clinical relevance, signals identified in genome-wide association studies (GWAS) may in principle unveil the roles of specific genes in normal biological processes. Despite the abundance of genetic risk loci identified for many immunological disorders and the deployment of fine-mapping approaches to identify the causal variant(s), only a minor fraction of risk loci has been linked to a causal gene, a specific immune cell type affected by the genetic variation, and ultimately to a mechanistically defined process. This notion is well exemplified by inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), which encompasses Crohn’s disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis. IBD is a multifactorial disease associated with increased colorectal cancer risk (Jess et al., 2012; Lutgens et al., 2008) and resulting from the interaction between genetic predisposition and local or environmental factors, with a critical role of T lymphocytes in disease pathogenesis (Baumgart and Sandborn, 2012; Maloy and Powrie, 2011). More than ∼200 IBD risk loci have been identified in GWAS cohorts (Huang et al., 2017; Jostins et al., 2012; Liu et al., 2015; Momozawa et al., 2018; Sazonovs et al., 2022), and many of them may point toward previously unrecognized regulators of T cell function. However, owing to the inherent challenges in deconvoluting GWAS signals, the majority of these loci remain orphan in terms of assignment to genes, immune cell types, or biological processes.Among the identified IBD risk loci, we were drawn to a 0.6-Mbp genomic region that has been linked to an increased risk of CD (Jostins et al., 2012; Liu et al., 2015; Momozawa et al., 2018; Schmiedel et al., 2018) as well as of other T cell–mediated autoimmune diseases such as type I diabetes and multiple sclerosis (Barrett et al., 2009; Beecham et al., 2013). Notably, this region contains a single gene, SKAP2 (Src kinase–associated phosphoprotein 2). SKAP2 is an adapter protein that shows widespread expression in immune cells and that was initially identified as a homolog of the lymphoid tissue-enriched protein SKAP1 (Liu et al., 1998; Marie-Cardine et al., 1998). However, in addition to different expression profiles, the two proteins share only 42.7% amino acid identity, arguing for functional specialization rather than redundancy.Both SKAP1 and SKAP2 have been involved in signal-dependent regulation of integrin-mediated adhesion and migration. Integrins are heterodimeric transmembrane proteins that enable adhesive interactions of T lymphocytes with endothelia and other immune cells, as well as extracellular matrix components (Springer, 1990). The critical role of adhesion-mediated mechanisms in T lymphocyte recruitment, migration, and activation is evident in both immune deficiencies and therapeutic interventions. In leukocyte adhesion deficiency (LAD), a group of heterogeneous immunodeficiency syndromes caused by mutations in integrins or associated proteins, defects in cell adhesion impair immune function (Hanna and Etzioni, 2012; Kishimoto et al., 1987). Conversely, integrin blockade has shown therapeutic effects in immune disease models (Gerber et al., 2013; Zundler et al., 2017) and human autoimmune disorders (Shimaoka and Springer, 2003; Slack et al., 2022). These observations highlight the necessity of precise regulation of these adhesion-dependent processes for normal immune system function.In the context of integrin regulation, SKAP1 was shown to positively, yet only transiently, impact clustering of lymphocyte function–associate antigen-1 (LFA-1), a heterodimer of the integrins ITGAL and ITGB2, during the very early phase of T cell activation (Jo et al., 2005; Wang et al., 2007; Wang et al., 2003), while being dispensable for chemokine-induced migration in the absence of TCR signaling (Wang et al., 2010). SKAP2 was instead shown to be required for integrin-induced cytoskeletal rearrangement in macrophages (Alenghat et al., 2012) and for integrin-mediated recruitment of neutrophils to inflamed tissues in vivo, to the point that loss of Skap2 was sufficient to induce a LAD-like phenotype in mice (Boras et al., 2017). Skap2 deletion also enhanced motility of dendritic cells, which was attributed to the loss of their ability to adhere to the extracellular matrix (Reinhold et al., 2009), thus suggesting an important role for SKAP2 in regulating immune cell adhesion. These initial data point toward an apparent dichotomy in the roles of these two proteins, with SKAP1 regulating T lymphocyte function and SKAP2 controlling adhesion and motility in myeloid cells. However, Skap2-deficient mice exhibited decreased severity of experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, a canonical T cell–mediated disease model (Togni et al., 2005), suggesting its possible involvement also in the regulation of T cells. Nevertheless, the role of SKAP2 in T cell activation as well as its interplay with SKAP1 remain unknown.Given the association of the SKAP2 locus with autoimmune diseases and the paucity of knowledge regarding the function of SKAP2 in T cells, we set out to investigate the regulation and impact of SKAP2 expression in human T lymphocytes. First, we found that the allelic variant associated with CD risk correlated with elevated SKAP2 protein expression in human T cells. Next, by generating T cells modified either to increase endogenous SKAP2 gene expression via CRISPR-mediated transcriptional activation (CRISPRa) or to abrogate SKAP2 expression through CRISPR-Cas9 genome editing, we found that SKAP2 plays a nonredundant role in regulating T cell adhesion and functions in response to TCR triggering. While SKAP1 was required for optimal T cell migration in response to chemokine signals, SKAP2 was uniquely responsible for modulating cell adhesion and even opposed migration. Analysis of the SKAP1 and SKAP2 protein interactomes in T cells revealed that, on the one hand, SKAP2 is part of a complex also containing SKAP1, but on the other, it also forms “private” interactions that likely account for the functional divergence of the two proteins. In addition, we found that T cell activation led to the upregulation of SKAP2, but not SKAP1, expression, followed by its caspase-mediated cleavage at later stages of activation. Such cleavage led to the release of the SKAP2 interactome, relieving T cell adhesive capacity and ultimately enabling migration.Overall, building on GWAS data, we uncovered a nonredundant, SKAP2-dependent mechanism of regulation of activation-induced T cell adhesion.ResultsA single-gene CD risk locus containing SKAP2We focused our attention on a 0.6-Mbp CD risk locus encompassing a CD-associated credible set of 194 noncoding genetic variants in linkage disequilibrium (Fig. 1 A). This locus has previously been linked to an increased risk of developing CD (Liu et al., 2015; Momozawa et al., 2018; Schmiedel et al., 2018), type I diabetes mellitus (Barrett et al., 2009), and multiple sclerosis (Beecham et al., 2013). No single causal variant could be linked to CD, even by fine-mapping analysis (Huang et al., 2017). However, the sum of all the 194 variants reached a posterior probability higher than 95% of being causal, suggesting the likely presence of the disease-causal variant(s). Moreover, since this region contains a single gene, SKAP2, this is likely to be the causative one. To gain insights into the regulatory impact of these 194 noncoding variants, we employed a genome essentiality metric, the noncoding essential regulation (ncER) score (Wells et al., 2019). This score estimates the likelihood of each noncoding variant being deleterious by integrating 38 features related to parameters such as evolutionary conservation, functional annotations, as well as regulatory element predictions. The score is provided as percentiles, with a value higher than 90 suggesting likely essentiality. We found that 32 of the 194 single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) had an ncER score >90 (Data S1), a finding also consistent with their association with regions conserved in evolution and predicted to have regulatory functions based on ReMap, an integrative analysis of hundreds of publicly available chromatin immunoprecipitation sequencing (ChIP-seq) datasets (Griffon et al., 2015) (Fig. 1 A). Hence, many of the SNPs in the SKAP2 locus are predicted to have regulatory consequences on gene expression. For instance, the gain of canonical motifs for the transcription factors NF-kB and IRF was selectively linked to the risk-associated allelic variant (Fig. 1 A).Figure 1.

Principal component analysis (PCA) and unsupervised clustering revealed that the SKAP2-CRISPRa samples clearly separated from NTCs in response to aCD3/aCD28 and aCD3/rICAM1 stimulation (Fig. 2 B). In both experimental groups, approximately twofold more differentially expressed genes were upregulated than downregulated (Fig. 2 B). Several of these differentially expressed genes were part of two main well-defined biological programs, namely cell cycle (e.g., CDC20, PLK1, CCNB1, CDK1, and NDC80) and T cell trafficking, adhesion, and activation (genes encoding integrins and chemokine receptors, such as integrin-α1 (ITGA1), integrin-β7 (ITGB7), CCR1, CCR2, CCR5, CXCR6, RAP1GAP2, and TNFSF4), suggesting an impact of increased SKAP2 levels on the control of activation-induced T cell proliferation (Fig. 2, C and D; and Data S2). Specifically, we show that genetically determined levels of SKAP2 expression control activation-induced T lymphocyte adhesion and migration. Consistent with their overall limited homology, SKAP2 acts in a nonredundant manner with SKAP1, and these two adapter proteins diverge in terms of regulation, protein interactomes and functions.Our findings can now be reconciled with previous data on the role of SKAP1 in T lymphocyte function within a coherent mechanistic framework. In the very early phases (minutes) of T cell activation, abundant SKAP1 levels are required for LFA-1 clustering and the initial formation of the T cell–APC conjugate (Wang et al., 2003), suggesting the possibility that SKAP1 might be required to induce such initial transient adhesion, while being insufficient to sustain T cell adhesion during activation. In fact, while SKAP1 is constitutively expressed and its levels do not change upon TCR engagement, SKAP2 is inducible. Hence, a coherent picture emerges in which SKAP1 is required for early events during T cell activation, such as the initial interaction with an APC. Upon productive T cell activation and upregulation, SKAP2 contributes to restraining T cell migration by increasing adhesion. Finally, SKAP2 proteolytic cleavage at a later stage enables T cell migration (model in Fig. S5 I). Although still in part speculative, this model is also consistent with the observation that T cell priming by dendritic cells requires three phases: the first one, lasting ∼8 h, is characterized by multiple transient encounters. Over time, T cells progressively decrease their motility. In the following ∼12 h, the second phase is characterized instead by stable contacts, followed by a third phase on day 2 that features reacquisition of high motility and proliferation (Mempel et al., 2004). It would be tempting to speculate that in T cells, the initial SKAP1-dependent, transient integrin activation is rapidly reversed in the absence of SKAP2 induction, leading to dissociation of T cells from APCs or endothelial cells.The lack of redundancy between SKAP1 and SKAP2 in the control of T cell responses is somewhat surprising given their ability to efficiently interact with the same TCR-proximal adapter, FYB (Griffiths et al., 2001; Liu et al., 1998; Timms et al., 1999; Voisinne et al., 2019). One crucial aspect in this regard is the fact that the two proteins are differentially regulated. The distinct functional roles of SKAP1 and SKAP2 can therefore be partly linked to their temporal regulation in T cells. Additionally, our proteomics data indicate the existence of distinct interactomes involving SKAP1 and SKAP2. On the one hand, SKAP2 is constitutively present together with SKAP1 in a trimolecular complex with FYB. Since SKAP2 possesses an N-terminal dimerization domain, it is tempting to speculate that the private SKAP2 interactome requires SKAP2 homodimerization enabled by its increased expression levels. Interestingly, a SKAP1 isoform lacking a single amino acid (V293) in a small motif required for the interaction with FYB was not co-immunoprecipitated with SKAP2 and instead appeared to account for a small SKAP1 private interactome. What is the functional relevance of this specific additional variant remains to be understood. Finally, we show that post-activation caspase-mediated cleavage of SKAP2 resulted in the release of interacting proteins and relieved its positive effect on T lymphocyte adhesion, enabling migration of activated T cells. Hence, caspase-mediated cleavage of SKAP2 has a specific role in the temporal progression of T cell responses.Our study characterized a mechanism central to the regulation of TCR-induced human T cell adhesion and migration. How can increased SKAP2 expression lead to an increased risk of CD and other autoimmune disorders? While still speculative, we envision a scenario in which genetic variation at the SKAP2 locus may enhance adhesion and tissue retention of activated and potentially pathogenic T cells, thereby promoting their abnormal compartmentalization and/or tissue accumulation (Fig. S5 J). By promoting an activation-induced adhesive state, genotype-driven increased SKAP2 expression may alter the normal recirculation of activated T cells and favor their persistence within disease-relevant immune compartments. If this turns out to be accurate using in vivo models, then levels of SKAP2 expression may also potentially define a mechanistically distinct subgroup of CD patients who may successfully respond to anti-integrin therapy. Indeed, given the increasing role of integrin antagonists in the therapeutic arsenal for IBD (Cherry et al., 2015), it will be important to determine if prior knowledge of the genetic status of the SKAP2 locus can be exploited to select patients who, because of high SKAP2 levels in T lymphocytes, may be particularly susceptible to the therapeutic effects of such inhibitors. One possibility is that homozygosity for the minor, alternative SKAP2 allelic variant would amplify adhesion-dependent programs and promote tissue retention of T lymphocytes that can potentially be reversed through the inhibitory effects of antagonists of α4β7 integrins, which are required for gut homing. Information regarding the genotype at the SKAP2 locus may thus assist in the therapeutic stratification of IBD patients. Retrospective and prospective studies exploring this possibility in conjunction with immunophenotyping of patients’ tissue samples are warranted.Overall, the impact of our work is several-fold. First, using GWAS data for autoimmune diseases as a starting point, our study identified a previously unknown component of the machinery linking TCR signal transduction to the control of integrin activity. While previous work demonstrated the role of SKAP1 in the regulation of early inside-out integrin events (Raab et al., 2010), no function has been assigned to SKAP2 in T cells. In this regard, it is important to stress that to date, GWAS studies have not reported SKAP1 as a risk locus for autoimmunity. Second, whereas the involvement of caspases in the control of T lymphocyte activation has been known for a long time (Newton and Strasser, 2003), our data shed light on a mechanism consisting of the termination of the communication between the TCR and the integrin system, which we propose may act as a switch enabling activated T cells to be relieved from strong adhesive interactions that would hinder their ability to migrate away from their site of activation. Finally, our study establishes a link between a previously identified autoimmune disease risk locus and a specific gene, a cell type, and a mechanism of action, a task whose notorious complexity hinders the understanding and the clinical translation of the vast majority of the findings reported in GWAS studies. Future studies will be needed to determine the practical relevance of our findings for IBD, and specifically CD patients.A limitation of this study is that due to their high number and contiguity, the specific SNP(s) responsible for the increased SKAP2 expression observed in individuals homozygous for the minor allele could not be identified, nor could the precise molecular mechanism underlying this regulatory effect be defined. The SKAP2 locus spans ∼0.6 Mbp and contains 194 noncoding variants in strong linkage disequilibrium, forming a large haplotype block. As a consequence, the association signal cannot be resolved to individual SNPs by statistical fine-mapping, and these variants are largely inherited together in the human population. Consistent with this genetic architecture, our experimental analyses relied on a representative SNP to stratify donors by genotype. In addition, the relatively low frequency of the minor allele, and the resulting small proportion of homozygous individuals, further limits the power to genetically disentangle candidate causal regulatory variants within this locus.
