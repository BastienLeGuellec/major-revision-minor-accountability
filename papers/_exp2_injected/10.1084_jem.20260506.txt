TITLE: Epitope-diverse autoantibodies induce neutrophil–epithelial niches driving kidney injury in ANCA-associated glomerulonephritis

ABSTRACT:
Nishide et al. identify neutrophil–epithelial niches associated with local kidney injury in ANCA-associated glomerulonephritis using single-cell–resolved spatial transcriptomics. Neutrophils in these niches show Fcγ receptor-related programs linked to epitope-diverse autoantibodies, while anti-MPO antibody combinations reproduce key pathological features in mice and engineered one-armed antibodies attenuate kidney injury in vivo.

BODY:
IntroductionAutoantibody-driven inflammation is a fundamental mechanism underlying organ-specific autoimmune diseases. Pathogenic antibodies trigger immune activation and tissue injury across multiple organs (Anders et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2023b; Lee and Kalluri, 2010). Despite advances in defining systemic immune dysregulation, how pathogenic autoantibodies initiate local tissue injury within affected organs remains incompletely understood.Recent spatial profiling studies of human tissues have revealed complex interactions between immune cells and parenchymal cells that accompany tissue remodeling and fibrosis (Krebs et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2025; Melo Ferreira et al., 2021). However, these studies typically capture established inflammatory lesions rather than the onset of disease, leaving the early events that initiate tissue injury largely unresolved (Daehn and Duffield, 2021). Addressing this challenge will require studying early-stage disease with mechanistic investigation to define how antibody-driven immune activation organizes into pathogenic cellular niches within tissues (Nishide et al., 2024).Autoimmune nephritides provide a clinically relevant system to investigate antibody-mediated tissue injury. Among these diseases, antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA)–associated glomerulonephritis arises as a primary renal manifestation rather than a secondary consequence of systemic inflammation, providing a well-defined context to dissect immune–tissue interactions (Foresto-Neto et al., 2024; Herrington et al., 2026; Kant et al., 2022; Sultana et al., 2025). Pathogenic autoantibodies against myeloperoxidase (MPO) or proteinase 3 activate neutrophils and induce effector responses including ROS production and neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation (Kitching et al., 2020). Anti-MPO antibodies recognize multiple epitopes on the MPO molecule, raising the possibility that epitope diversity may shape neutrophil activation during disease (Gou et al., 2013; Roth et al., 2013; Suzuki et al., 2007). Understanding how pathogenic immune responses are organized within tissues is essential not only for defining the mechanisms that initiate organ damage but also for enabling mechanism-based therapeutic interventions.In this study, we combine single-cell–resolved spatial transcriptomics with mechanistic modeling to investigate how pathogenic autoantibodies initiate kidney injury in autoimmune glomerulonephritis. Spatial profiling of kidney biopsies from new-onset, treatment-naïve patients with MPO-ANCA–associated glomerulonephritis identifies peri-glomerular inflammatory niches composed of injured proximal tubular epithelial cells and infiltrating immune populations. Mechanistic experiments demonstrate that combinations of anti-MPO antibodies recognizing distinct epitopes enhance neutrophil activation through Fcγ receptor (FcγR) signaling and induce crescentic glomerulonephritis in vivo. In addition, fibroblasts expressing fibroblast activation protein (FAP+ fibroblasts) (You et al., 2023) were significantly increased in patient kidneys (Fig. 1 D). Fibroblast-focused analysis identified FAP+ fibroblasts as a distinct population in patient kidneys (Fig. 1 E), showing elevated expression of extracellular matrix–associated genes, such as COL5A1/2 and POSTN (Fig. 1 F). The UMAPs for individual participants are shown in the deposited Figshare dataset described in the Data availability section. Compared with healthy donors, patient kidneys showed a broad expansion of immune cell populations, including B cells, CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, natural killer cells, plasmablasts, plasma cells, and granulocytes (Fig. 1 I). Among the macrophage subsets, IL1B+ macrophages were reduced, whereas CD163+ macrophages and macrophages expressing triggering receptor expressed on myeloid cells 2 (TREM2+ macrophages) were significantly increased in patient kidneys (Fig. 1 I). Macrophage-focused analysis identified TREM2+ macrophages as a distinct population enriched in patient kidneys (Fig. 1 J). TREM2+ macrophages showed elevated expression of LIPA and ANXA2 (Fig. 1 K), consistent with macrophage programs related to lipid handling and tissue remodeling (Jaitin et al., 2019). An expanded panel of representative marker gene expression patterns across macrophage clusters is shown in Fig. S1 E. Highly expressed genes identified by DEG analysis of each macrophage subset are listed in Table S2. Module scoring analysis revealed similarities between TREM2+ macrophages and scar-associated macrophages (SAMs) (Fig. 1 L), a profibrotic macrophage population associated with tissue remodeling and fibrosis in chronically injured organs (Fabre et al., 2023; Ramachandran et al., 2019). Together, these data demonstrate the extensive reorganization of renal cell composition in MPO-ANCA–associated glomerulonephritis. PTCs were classified into three sequential states according to injury progression: Normal_PTC, Early_Damaged_PTC, and Terminal_Damaged_PTC (Fig. 4 A). During the transition from Normal_PTC to Early_Damaged_PTC, genes involved in cellular stress and damage sensing, including CDKN1A and TP53, were upregulated. Concomitantly, the expression of chemokine genes, such as CXCL6 and CXCL16, increased in Early_Damaged_PTCs (Fig. 4 A). Peri-glomerular PTC stress responses shape a chemokine-enriched niche that accumulates immune cells, including activated neutrophils characterized by T2ISG expression and enhanced FcγR signaling. A pathogenic anti-MPO antibody–driven mouse model recapitulates these features, and selective blockade of FcγR-dependent neutrophil activation using one-armed antibodies attenuates glomerular injury in vivo.DiscussionAutoimmune glomerulonephritis does not result from random immune infiltration but is organized via spatially defined renal niches that coordinate immune, epithelial, and stromal activation (Lake et al., 2023; Melo Ferreira et al., 2021; Reck et al., 2025). By reconstructing glomerular niche states using pseudotime analysis, our study suggests that pathogenic events may emerge in peri-glomerular microenvironments before overt glomerular inflammation becomes apparent. Spatial maps revealed that stressed epithelial cells activate chemokine programs that recruit and retain neutrophils, T cells, and B cells, thereby establishing a permissive immune niche that precedes glomerular lesion progression.PTC injury onset can be attributed to diverse insults, including ischemia, exposure to damage-associated molecular patterns, and release of inflammatory mediators by infiltrating immune cells (Tang et al., 2019). A key finding of this study was that p53 pathway activation in Early_Damaged_PTCs coincided with increased immune cell accumulation around injured tubules. This association suggests that epithelial stress may contribute actively to local inflammation rather than merely serve as a marker of tubular damage. Tubular p53 activation has been shown to promote inflammatory cytokine transcription through the SIRT1–p65/NF-κB axis (Fu et al., 2022), whereas PTC-specific deletion of p53 attenuates ischemia-induced acute kidney injury and the accompanying inflammatory responses (Ying et al., 2014). These findings support the possibility that p53 activation in Early_Damaged_PTCs contributes to a local cytokine and chemokine milieu that facilitates immune cell recruitment. The ligand–receptor interactions detected within Damaged_PTC niches are consistent with this model, suggesting functional cross talk between epithelial injury and immune cell localization in ANCA-associated glomerulonephritis.More broadly, these epithelial–immune interactions place the pathogenic trigger of ANCA-associated glomerulonephritis within the biology of maladaptive tubular repair. Spatial transcriptomic studies of mouse acute kidney injury have identified fibro-inflammatory niches comprising failed-repair PTCs, fibroblasts, and infiltrating immune populations (Xuanyuan et al., 2025). Within this shared injury program, CXCL16 produced by myeloid cells may recruit or retain CXCR6+ T cells near damaged tubules. Experimental evidence indicates that this axis promotes renal T cell accumulation, PTC death, fibrosis, and loss of kidney function during maladaptive repair (Li et al., 2026). Thus, CXCL16–CXCR6-dependent T cell localization may represent a general mechanism of persistent tubular injury rather than a response specific to ANCA-associated glomerulonephritis. By contrast, CXCL6–CXCR1/2-dependent recruitment of FcγR-responsive neutrophils to Damaged_PTC niches may be more directly linked to the antibody-dependent pathogenesis of AAV. Together, these findings suggest that ANCA-associated glomerulonephritis shares features of a conserved maladaptive repair program, while its disease-specific inflammatory character may be shaped by antibody-driven neutrophil recruitment and activation.Neutrophils within Damaged_PTC niches represent a tissue-damaging subset characterized by T2ISGs and enhanced FcγR signaling (Nishide et al., 2025). Although the temporal hierarchy between neutrophil activation and epithelial stress cannot be fully determined, epithelial stress and neutrophil influx may initially arise in parallel and subsequently reinforce each other in a feed-forward amplification loop that accelerates tissue injury. In clinical practice, conventional clinical markers are poorly suited for detecting early fibrogenic shifts (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes, 2024; Levin and Stevens, 2011). In our study, peri-glomerular Damaged_PTC signatures were correlated with reduced eGFR at shorter disease durations, suggesting that they may serve as indicators of prefibrotic states that precede tissue remodeling. However, these patient-level correlations should be interpreted cautiously because of spatial heterogeneity within biopsy samples, which may be affected by compositional changes in lesion-associated populations. Validation in larger cohorts will be required to confirm these associations.Along the trajectory of disease progression, we identified two fibrogenic cell populations, TREM2+ macrophages and FAP+ fibroblasts, that expand within advanced glomerular niches. TREM2+ macrophages share transcriptional features with SAMs described in liver fibrosis, whereas FAP+ fibroblasts adopt programs resembling mCAFs, highlighting conserved profibrotic transcriptional states across tissues. Spatially, these populations accumulated predominantly in the crescentic glomeruli, and their abundance correlated with the extent of crescent formation, indicating that fibrogenic remodeling emerged as a downstream consequence of earlier immune–epithelial interactions.To mechanistically validate this neutrophil–epithelial axis, we developed monoclonal anti-MPO antibodies that directly activated neutrophils and established an antibody-induced model of glomerulonephritis. Previous attempts to induce glomerulonephritis in wild-type mice using single monoclonal anti-MPO antibodies have been limited, suggesting that antibody binding alone is insufficient to trigger the disease (Huang et al., 2023a; Shochet et al., 2020). In our study, the enhanced disease induction by the A24/A46/B88 combination may reflect an antibody-binding geometry that favors FcγR clustering. Although mAb-A46 and mAb-B88 bound overlapping regions on the MPO heavy-chain surface, cryo-EM analysis showed that they recognized distinct residues and approached MPO from different angles. We therefore speculate that these differences alter the orientation of exposed Fc domains and thereby facilitate productive FcγR engagement, consistent with previous evidence that Fc receptor activation depends on both Fc valency and multivalent Fc organization (Klaassen et al., 1988; Ortiz et al., 2016). Supporting this model, one-armed anti-MPO antibodies that preserved antigen binding while minimizing FcγR engagement failed to activate neutrophils and effectively attenuated glomerular injury, highlighting FcγR-dependent neutrophil activation as a tractable therapeutic target.This study has several limitations. First, human biopsy data represent cross-sectional snapshots, and pseudotime analyses provide computational inferences rather than direct temporal measurements. Longitudinal spatial sampling is required to resolve causal relationships definitively. Second, the antibody-induced model relies on strain-specific innate immune responsiveness, with severe disease development in 129S6 mice that exhibit heightened neutrophil reactivity (Jones et al., 2020; Mekada and Yoshiki, 2021). Finally, the three one-armed antibodies used in this study should not be interpreted as covering all pathogenic epitopes within the polyclonal anti-MPO repertoire. Rather, occupancy of pathogenicity-enriched Hb/Hc regions may interfere with antibody-binding geometries that favor FcγR-dependent neutrophil activation, even when other anti-MPO antibodies remain able to bind MPO. Further epitope-resolved analyses will be required to determine how the geometric arrangement of antibody-binding sites on MPO influences FcγR-dependent neutrophil activation.In conclusion, this study integrated spatial pathology, mechanistic modeling, and therapeutic intervention to demonstrate that peri-glomerular proximal tubules act as central hubs connecting neutrophil activation to progressive immune and stromal remodeling. The interruption of the earliest events of neutrophil activation may represent an effective strategy for preventing fibrogenic progression. More broadly, cross-disease spatial analyses may reveal conserved epithelial stress circuits underlying immune-mediated tissue injury and identify opportunities for early mechanism-based therapeutic interventions.Materials and methodsStudy participants and sample preparationAll clinical samples were collected from study participants who provided informed consent, in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.
