TITLE: Plasma proteomic signatures of subclinical atherosclerosis progression for cardiovascular risk stratification: A cohort study

ABSTRACT:
BackgroundExisting risk stratification tools inadequately identify individuals with subclinical atherosclerosis (SA) who could benefit from early intervention. This study aimed to develop protein risk scores (ProtRSs) for SA progression and assess their incremental value for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk prediction.Methods and findingsIn a community cohort consisting of 903 individuals undergoing repeated SA assessments (coronary artery calcium [CAC] or carotid plaque) over 6 years (2008–2009 to 2013–2014), protein biomarkers were selected from a panel of 117 proteins using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression to construct SA ProtRSs for CAC and plaque progression, respectively, and their predictive abilities for incident ASCVD risk were evaluated based on a guideline-recommended traditional risk score in a case-cohort of 1,835 individuals (2007–2008 to 2018–2021, median follow-up of 10.4 years). The ProtRSs each included 14 proteins for CAC and plaque progression, explaining 17.1% and 18.4% of respective variances. Effect sizes of these proteins on SA progression showed strong concordance with those on incident ASCVD (r  > 0.75). Each standard deviation increase in CAC and plaque ProtRSs was associated with hazard ratios of 1.36 (95% confidence interval [CI] [1.20,1.54]) and 1.38 (95% CI [1.23,1.54]) for ASCVD risk, respectively. Addition of the SA ProtRSs to the traditional risk score yielded a net reclassification improvement of 0.127 (p < 0.001). Importantly, 18.6% of traditional intermediate-risk individuals were reclassified into the high-risk category (10-year ASCVD event rate: 10.9%), indicating the need for preventive interventions, whereas 32.8% of traditional high-risk individuals were identified as having an even higher rate (13.7%), suggesting the requirement for intensified management. The main limitation is that the predictive value of ProtRSs warrants further large-scale external validation.ConclusionsSA ProtRSs of coronary calcification and carotid plaque progression may provide additional value over traditional risk assessment, highlighting their potential in refining risk stratification and guiding targeted intervention strategies.

BODY:
IntroductionAtherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) remains a major contributor to global disease burden and mortality [1,2]. While current clinical guidelines advocate for risk assessment tools based on established cardiovascular risk factors in primary prevention, emerging evidence reveals significant limitations in traditional risk stratification [3,4]. Specifically, some individuals deemed at intermediate- or even low-risk by traditional risk scores may still harbor a substantial atherosclerotic burden and be susceptible to disease progression [5]. This gap underscores the importance of detecting subclinical atherosclerosis (SA), an early and pivotal stage of ASCVD in which preventive measures could have a stronger impact [6]. Currently, guidelines recommend imaging-based coronary artery calcium (CAC) scoring as an enhancing factor of ASCVD risk assessment [7,8]. However, the feasibility of widespread CAC scanning is limited by restricted access to computed tomography (CT) equipment, radiation exposure, and cost-effectiveness concerns [7]. Thus, the development of sensitive and widely accessible approaches to assess atherosclerotic burden is urgently needed.Recent advances in proteomics have enabled large-scale population studies to quantify plasma protein levels [9]. Several European cohort studies have discovered protein biomarkers associated with atherosclerotic lesions, such as growth/differentiation factor 15 (GDF-15) and T-cell immunoglobulin and mucin domain (TIM)-1 [10,11]. However, most of these studies were based on a cross-sectional design, making it difficult to determine whether protein alterations precede the development of atherosclerosis or merely reflect its concurrent pathology. This limitation underscores the need to identify protein biomarkers driving SA progression in prospective cohorts, which would offer predictive value for future ASCVD events [12]. Moreover, the extent to which these protein biomarkers improve risk stratification beyond traditional scoring remains understudied, particularly in non-European populations.To address these gaps, this study aimed to (1) identify candidate plasma proteins driving the development of SA among 903 Chinese adults who underwent repeated CAC or carotid ultrasound measurements over a 6-year period, (2) incorporate these proteins into protein risk scores (ProtRSs) for SA progression, and (3) evaluate whether these ProtRSs can enhance ASCVD prediction beyond traditional risk scores in a prospective case-cohort of 1,835 participants.MethodsEthics statementAll studies were approved by the Institutional Review Board at Fuwai Hospital (Approval number 2022-1665) and written informed consent was obtained from each participant prior to data collection.Study design and populationThe study design is illustrated in Fig 1, and a prospective protocol, including changes made in response to the reviewers’ comments, is presented in S1 Text. Based on a discovery population with repeated SA examinations, protein biomarkers were selected by least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression and used to construct the ProtRSs for SA progression. The predictive performance of the ProtRSs for incident ASCVD risk was assessed in a case-cohort.10.1371/journal.pmed.1005265.g001Fig 1Flowchart of the study.Abbreviations: SA, subclinical atherosclerosis; LASSO, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator; CAC, coronary artery calcium; ASCVD, atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease; ProtRS, protein risk score; NRI, net reclassification improvement; Int, intermediate.For the discovery cohort, 1,087 participants from the Beijing Community-Based Cohort of Atherosclerosis Study [13] were initially enrolled in 2008–2009 and were followed up at 2013–2014. After excluding 184 participants without protein data, 903 participants were retained for subsequent analysis. Among them, 815 and 781 were available for CAC and plaque measurements, respectively (Fig AA in S1 Appendix).For the validation cohort, participants were included based on a case-cohort design nested within the International Collaborative Study of Cardiovascular Disease in Asia (InterASIA) [14] and the China Multi-Center Collaborative Study of Cardiovascular Epidemiology (ChinaMUCA) cohorts [15]. At baseline (2007–2008), participants free of ASCVD were enrolled. The case-cohort was constructed by including all incident ASCVD cases that developed during follow-up (up to 2018–2021) and a random 10% sub-cohort of the overall participants. This initial case-cohort comprised 1,449 sub-cohort individuals and 501 additional incident cases after a median follow-up period of 10.4 years. After excluding 115 participants without protein data, 1,835 individuals were retained for the analysis (Fig AB in S1 Appendix). Specifically, the InterASIA cohort included 758 sub-cohort individuals (44 ASCVD cases) and 272 additional cases while the ChinaMUCA cohort included 633 sub-cohort individuals (21 ASCVD cases) and 172 additional cases outside the sub-cohort.This study is reported as per the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guideline (S1 Checklist) and the Transparent Reporting Of a Multivariate Prediction Model (TRIPOD) guideline (S2 Checklist).Data collection and outcome definitionBaseline information, including demographics, lifestyle risk factors, medical history, and laboratory variables, was collected by trained staff through physical examinations and questionnaire interviews as previously published [15]. Blood samples were collected after an overnight fast of at least 10 hours, processed for plasma separation, and stored at −80 °C (S1 Text).CAC scanning was performed at Fuwai Hospital using a LightSpeed VCT 64-slice CT scanner (General Electric Healthcare, U.S.) and CAC was scored using the Agatston approach [16]. Baseline CAC was classified into three categories: CAC = 0 (not detectable), 0 < CAC < 100 (mild), and CAC ≥ 100 (moderate to severe). Based on previously widely used criteria, CAC progression was defined as one of the following conditions: (1) CAC > 0 at follow-up for participants with baseline CAC = 0; (2) an annualized CAC change ≥10 at follow-up for those with baseline 0 < CAC < 100; (3) an annualized percent change in CAC ≥ 10% for those with baseline CAC ≥ 100 [17–19].Carotid ultrasound was conducted using an iE33 ultrasound station (Philips Healthcare, Netherland) equipped with a 7.5-MHz linear array probe. Carotid plaque was defined as a focal intima-media thickness exceeding 1.5 mm or focal thickening at least 50% greater than the surrounding wall in the common carotid artery, carotid bifurcation, and internal carotid artery. The total number of plaques in bilateral carotid arteries was counted and plaque progression was defined as an increase in the number of plaques at follow-up compared to baseline according to the Multi-Ethnic Study of Atherosclerosis (MESA) study [20].Incident ASCVD was defined as the first occurrence of nonfatal acute myocardial infarction, coronary heart disease (CHD) death, or fatal or nonfatal ischemic stroke (IS). For both baseline and follow-up, the outcomes were collected via interviews with participants or their proxies, and validated using hospital records or death certificates, as described in the S1 Text. The absence of ASCVD at baseline was defined as no clinical diagnosis of incident ASCVD by a physician, nor self-reported incident ASCVD during interviews with participants or their proxies before 2007–2008. Person-years of follow-up for each participant were calculated as the time interval between the baseline and the earliest occurrence of ASCVD, death, or last follow-up, whichever came first.Proteomics measurementsPlasma levels of 117 proteins were quantified in this study, which are involved in ASCVD-related pathways such as inflammation, immune response, and lipid metabolism (Table A in S2 Appendix). Protein measurement was performed using three platforms: the Olink Cardiovascular-III panel using proximity extension assay technology for 92 proteins [21], an automated ELISA-based microfluidic system (ELLA, ProteinSimple) for 14 inflammatory proteins, and clinical biochemistry assays on Beckman Coulter AU680 for 11 metabolic proteins. All measured concentrations underwent Z-score standardization (mean = 0, standard deviation [SD] = 1). Principal component analysis revealed no platform-specific clustering patterns (Fig B in S1 Appendix).Protein risk scoreLASSO regression with 10-fold cross-validation was performed in 1,000 iterations (randomly selected 90% sample size) to select proteins associated with CAC or plaque progression, adjusting for age and sex. Proteins retaining nonzero coefficients in ≥ 900 iterations for each outcome were subsequently incorporated into two distinct ProtRSs through logistic regression modeling. Final ProtRS values were calculated on the logit scale as the sum of each protein’s regression coefficient multiplied by its standardized plasma level.Statistical analysisData are presented as mean (SD) or median (quartile 1 [Q1], quartile 3 [Q3]) for continuous variables and as frequency (%) for categorical variables.Based on percentiles of ProtRSs in the population [22], participants were categorized into: low (bottom 20%), intermediate (middle 60%), and high (top 20%) groups. Log-binomial regressions were used to estimate relative risks (RRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) of ProtRSs for CAC and plaque progression, respectively, adjusting for traditional cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk factors including baseline age, sex, urbanization, smoking status, waist circumference, systolic blood pressure, antihypertensive therapy, total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, and diabetes. The ptrend was calculated by taking the medians of each protein risk category (low, intermediate, and high) as continuous numeric variables with the hypothesis that the risk of CAC and plaque progression increases with the higher risk categories in protein risk categories.To evaluate the predictive accuracy of SA ProtRSs for the risk of SA progression, area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) values were estimated for each ProtRS, and their comparisons with a reference model including the above-mentioned traditional CVD risk factors were assessed by DeLong’s test [23]. Calibration plots were used to assess the agreement between the observed risk of SA progression and the predicted risk by traditional risk factors with the addition of ProtRSs. To further identify proteins with biological relevance, logistic regression models with forward method were used, and Over-Representation Analysis (ORA) was performed to investigate which particular pathways from the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) were enriched among the biologically related-proteins.For the risk of incident ASCVD, Cox regressions were used to estimate hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% CIs of ProtRSs in the case-cohort. We hypothesized that proteins exerting stronger effects on SA progression would confer a higher risk of incident ASCVD, and Pearson correlation analysis was performed to compare the effect sizes of these proteins between SA progression and ASCVD incidence. The incremental value of ProtRSs was assessed over the guideline-recommended Prediction for Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease Risk in China (China-PAR) risk score, which is a sex-specific risk equation for the 10-year risk of ASCVD incorporating age, geographic region, urbanization, systolic blood pressure, antihypertensive treatment, total cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol, waist circumference, current smoking status, diabetes mellitus, family history of ASCVD and available interaction terms for age with risk factors that met predefined statistical criteria [15]. Due to the absence of hemorrhagic stroke in the current study outcome compared with the original China-PAR risk score, when calculating the absolute observed ASCVD event rate, the baseline survival function (S0) was re-estimated among the entire cohort (n = 14,496) and thresholds of < 5%, 5%–10%, and ≥ 10% were set to define China-PAR low-, intermediate-, and high-risk groups.The prediction performance of ProtRSs and their added value to the reference risk score were evaluated by Harrell’s C-index through 1,000 bootstrapping iterations [25,26]. In addition, categorical net reclassification improvement (NRI) was estimated based on cut-off values of 5% and 10%, and the p-value of categorical NRI was calculated through 1,000 bootstrapping iterations. Cases that developed outside the sub-cohort after 10 years were excluded according to methods applied to case-cohort design [25]. To explore the clinical implications for intermediate-risk individuals, the NRI was further calculated among participants with a China-PAR risk of 5–10% [27].Furthermore, risk stratification achieved by SA ProtRSs beyond the China-PAR risk score was evaluated within a 10-year timeframe by estimating the group-specific observed risks. The SA protein risk category was combined as high (high CAC and high plaque ProtRS), intermediate (only high CAC or only high plaque ProtRS), and low (low or intermediate CAC and low or intermediate plaque ProtRS). The observed risk of incident ASCVD for each category was estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and a weighting of 1/(sampling proportion) was applied to the sub-cohort individuals to compensate for the over-representation of cases.All statistical tests with p < 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Multiple testing was adjusted using the false discovery rate (FDR) method. Statistical analyses were performed in R version 4.4.3.Artificial intelligence statementNo artificial intelligence tools or technologies were used to generate, edit, or analyze the study, data, or article content. Abbreviations: ASCVD, atherosclerotic cardiovascular diseases; ProtRS, protein risk score; CAC, coronary artery calcium.DiscussionLeveraging prospective cohorts with repeated measurements of CAC and carotid plaque, we characterized plasma protein profiles underlying subclinical atherosclerotic progression. The derived ProtRSs demonstrated robust linear associations with incident ASCVD and conferred modest incremental gains in risk stratification beyond conventional guideline-based risk assessment. In particular, reclassification improvements were most pronounced in guideline-defined intermediate-risk individuals who may benefit greatly from early intervention. Our findings advance the understanding of plasma proteins for ASCVD primary prevention through three key aspects.First, we systematically selected protein drivers underlying SA progression, thereby elucidating the biological processes underlying CAC and carotid plaque. Unlike previous cross-sectional investigations [11,28–30], our prospective study with a median 6-year follow-up enabled identification of proteins that precede and potentially mediate atherosclerotic progression. Furthermore, forward stepwise logistic regression analyses verified that most LASSO-selected proteins might be biologically relevant to SA progression. We observed shared proteins implicated in both CAC and plaque progression (CTSD, sdLDL, and PON3), which were involved in well-established atherosclerotic mechanisms, including autophagy-related inflammation, lipid deposition, metabolic regulation, and immune modulation [31–37]. Meanwhile, KEGG pathway analysis further demonstrated that protein signatures for plaque progression and CAC progression converged on inflammation and cholesterol homeostasis regulation pathways that drive atherogenesis, including IL-8, CCL-24, MCP-1, and low-density lipoprotein receptor [38–41].Moreover, we uncovered multiple previously underrecognized protein biomarkers involved in atherogenesis. KLK6 displayed protective associations with CAC progression, consistent with its established neuroprotective properties [42]. Transferrin receptor protein 1 (TR), a key regulator of ferroptosis, was also identified as an important protein involved in pathologic processes of atherosclerosis [43]. Additionally, osteoprotegerin (OPG) and CPB1 were associated with plaque progression, further highlighting the roles of vascular calcification and proteolytic remodeling in atherosclerotic pathogenesis [44,45]. A previous phenome-wide mendelian randomization study has clarified the causal relationships of these proteins with cardiovascular disease-related traits, including KLK6 with body mass index, CPB1 with myocardial infarction, as well as TR with low-density lipoprotein cholesterol and hemoglobin A1c (Table K in S2 Appendix and S1 Text) [46].Second, we developed CAC- and plaque-specific ProtRSs that demonstrated robust associations with subclinical SA progression and subsequent incident ASCVD. In our cohort with a mean age of 54 years, CAC progression was observed in 33.4% of participants and carotid plaque progression in 22.0%, underscoring the clinical need for sensitive biomarkers to identify individuals prone to progressive vascular disease. The constructed ProtRSs explained nearly 20% of the phenotypic variance in SA progression and corresponded to a 1.25-fold elevated risk of progressive atherosclerosis.Most prior protein-based prediction studies exclusively focused on overt cardiovascular events, with insufficient attention paid to early subclinical vascular remodeling [47]. Furthermore, conventional cardiovascular biomarkers, such as N-terminal prohormone brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), apolipoprotein(a) (LPA) and proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9), showed no significant associations with SA progression in our analyses. Accordingly, our study addresses this prominent knowledge gap by establishing atherosclerosis-tailored risk scores, which are uniquely valuable for monitoring early vascular disease progression. Of critical importance, proteins displaying stronger associations with subclinical atherosclerotic progression also exhibited more pronounced links to incident ASCVD, with high phenotypic coherence (CAC progression: r 0.77, p = 0.001; plaque progression: r 0.85, p < 0.001). This biological consistency supports a plausible causal chain whereby protein-mediated alterations in early atherosclerosis ultimately translate to future clinical cardiovascular events, in line with analogous mechanistic concordance reported in prior biomarker studies [48,49]. In line with this framework, each SD increase in ProtRSs was associated with over 35% higher ASCVD risk. Individuals with high ProtRS risk presented a 3-fold higher incidence of ASCVD relative to those with low ProtRS risk, and those with high ProtRS risk demonstrated a 4- to 5-fold elevated risk of CHD compared with their low-risk counterparts. Collectively, these findings establish a biologically coherent link between protein-driven subclinical atherosclerotic progression and long-term adverse cardiovascular outcomes.Third, the ProtRSs could confer incremental value for ASCVD risk prediction and reclassification beyond conventional risk assessment. Previous evidence reported that 10%, 33%, and 55% of individuals categorized as low-, intermediate-, and high-risk by traditional risk algorithms exhibited generalized SA, respectively [50]. This highlights the inherent limitations of conventional risk scores in identifying subclinical atherosclerotic burden, leaving considerable residual cardiovascular risk unaddressed in primary ASCVD prevention [51,52]. Previous proteomic studies have explored the utility of circulating protein biomarkers and found modest improvement when adding plasma proteins to traditional risk scores in primary prevention settings [53,54]. For instance, in a UK cohort comprising 38,380 participants with 2,919 proteins quantified, the addition of 114 selected proteins to Systematic COronary Risk Evaluation 2 (SCORE2) yielded mild improvements in 10-year major cardiovascular events (MACE) risk prediction (AUC 0.771; NRI 0.140) [54]. In comparison, our analysis revealed an overall significant reclassification improvement with a total NRI of 0.127, reflecting appropriate reallocation of participants into clinically meaningful risk strata. Notably, the reclassification benefit was markedly pronounced among guideline-defined intermediate-risk individuals, with an NRI of 0.416. However, adding ProtRSs also incorrectly reclassified 20 of 106 future cases from intermediate to low-risk, potentially leading to missed preventive opportunities.Absolute risk stratification further revealed that intermediate-risk individuals reclassified to high-risk by the ProtRS exhibited a 10-year ASCVD incidence of 10.9%, surpassing the guideline-recommended threshold for initiating pharmacological intervention. Accordingly, the ProtRS may act as a valuable risk enhancer to guide clinical decision-making among individuals with ambiguous baseline risk under current guidelines. Furthermore, within the conventionally high-risk population, the ProtRS identified a high-risk subgroup with an elevated 10-year event rate of 13.7%, supporting the need for more aggressive preventive strategies, particularly among Chinese populations characterized by relatively poor adherence to cardiovascular interventions [55,56]. Taken together, these findings indicate that integrating ProtRSs into traditional risk scores can refine risk stratification and identify individuals with SA who may benefit from earlier intervention.Overall, our study adds new insights into the limited evidence regarding proteins contributing to SA progression. The CAC- and plaque-specific protein risk scores demonstrated robust predictive capacity for SA progression, and their associations with incident ASCVD further support the clinical relevance of these proteomic markers in longitudinal follow-up. However, our findings should be interpreted in the context of certain limitations. First, despite the relatively broad 117-protein panel adopted, it may not encompass the full spectrum of the atherosclerotic proteome. Second, although pathway enrichment analysis supported the protein-disease associations, the causal biological mechanisms require functional validation through mechanistic investigations. Third, further large-scale external validation across populations and cost-effectiveness evaluation are needed before these protein risk scores can be implemented in clinical practice.In conclusion, the atherosclerosis-specific protein risk scores reflecting coronary calcification and carotid plaque progression confer incremental ASCVD predictive value over conventional risk assessment tools.
