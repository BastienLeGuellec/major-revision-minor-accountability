TITLE: Disitamab Vedotin Plus Tislelizumab in Transurethrally Unresectable, ERBB2-Positive, Non–Muscle-Invasive Bladder Cancer

ABSTRACT:
This nonrandomized clinical trial examines the efficacy of disitamab vedotin plus tislelizumab for transurethrally unresectable, ERBB2-positive, very high-risk, non–muscle-invasive bladder cancer in adults who were ineligible for or declined radical cystectomy.

BODY:
IntroductionNon–muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) is commonly managed with complete transurethral resection of bladder tumor (TURBT) followed by risk-adapted intravesical therapy. Very high-risk (VHR) NMIBC is a guideline-defined risk category that includes features such as extensive high-grade T1 (T1HG) disease, T1HG with carcinoma in situ (CIS), variant histology, lymphovascular invasion, or bacille Calmette-Guérin (BCG)–unresponsive disease. Patients with transurethrally unresectable VHR NMIBC are generally recommended to undergo immediate radical cystectomy when feasible. More than 60% of patients with VHR NMIBC are medically ineligible for upfront radical cystectomy or decline the procedure, and for these patients established nonsurgical treatment options are limited.In the previous phase 2 TRUCE-02 trial (NCT04730232), tislelizumab plus low-dose nab-paclitaxel achieved a 62.7% complete response (CR) rate of high-risk disease in patients with transurethrally unresectable VHR NMIBC, establishing proof of concept that systemic therapy could achieve meaningful disease clearance in this setting. Tislelizumab is an anti–programmed cell death 1 protein (PD-1) antibody that restores antitumor immune responses by blocking PD-1–mediated immune suppression. Building on these findings, the emergence of ERBB2 (formerly HER2)–directed antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) provided an opportunity to further develop biomarker-selected systemic therapy in this setting. ERBB2 positivity is reported in approximately 40% to 50% of bladder cancers. Disitamab vedotin (DV) is an ERBB2-targeted ADC that delivers a cytotoxic payload to ERBB2-expressing tumor cells, and DV combined with a PD-1 blockade has shown promising clinical activity in advanced urothelial carcinoma. Together, the biologic complementarity and clinical activity observed in advanced disease provided the rationale for evaluating DV plus tislelizumab in patients with transurethrally unresectable ERBB2-positive VHR NMIBC.In this study, we describe the results of the single-arm, phase 2 TRUCE-04 trial, in which we evaluated disitamab vedotin plus tislelizumab in patients with transurethrally unresectable ERBB2-positive VHR NMIBC. We hypothesized that this regimen could clear residual high-risk disease in 70% of patients, informed by prior response rates of 56% to 75%.MethodsThis nonrandomized clinical trial was reviewed and approved by the institutional review board of the Second Hospital of Tianjin Medical University and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and International Conference on Harmonization Good Clinical Practice guidelines. All patients provided written informed consent. The study followed the Transparent Reporting of Evaluations With Nonrandomized Designs (TREND) reporting guidelines.PatientsThe TRUCE-04 trial was developed after the phase 2 TRUCE-02 trial and used the same core eligibility criteria for transurethrally unresectable VHR NMIBC, with the addition of ERBB2 expression requirements. Race and ethnicity data were collected in routine demographic documentation and were available from the participants’ CRFs and medical records, although these variables were not separately prespecified for subgroup analyses in the protocol. Patients were assessed for eligibility from August 2022 to May 2024; eligible participants were adults diagnosed with transurethrally unresectable VHR NMIBC who were ineligible for or declined radical cystectomy. The definition of transurethrally unresectable bladder tumor required visually incomplete transurethral resection or high-volume tumor, confirmed by cystoscopy, multipoint bladder biopsies, and independent verification by at least 2 senior urologists. High-volume tumors were defined as single or confluent lesions greater than 5 cm in diameter or multifocal lesions accounting for greater than 30% of the total bladder surface area. Pre-enrollment screening for VHR NMIBC followed the 2019 European Association of Urology criteria as follows: encompassing multiple, large, or recurrent T1HG tumors; T1HG with concurrent bladder CIS; T1HG with CIS in the prostatic urethra; variant histology of urothelial carcinoma; lymphovascular invasion; or BCG-unresponsive disease.Additional key eligibility criteria included histologically confirmed urothelial carcinoma (dominant component ≥50% for mixed histology), ERBB2-positive (immunohistochemistry [IHC] 2+ or 3+), Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status 0 to 2, adequate organ function, and positive postoperative urine cytology within 12 weeks of diagnostic TURBT. The detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria are provided in the trial protocol in Supplement 1.Study DesignThe TRUCE-04 trial was an open-label, single-arm, phase 2 pilot study evaluating the efficacy of DV combined with tislelizumab in patients with ERBB2-positive VHR NMIBC. The primary end point was the CR rate of high-risk disease, defined as the absence of T1 tumors, high-grade tumors, CIS, and progressive disease (assessed approximately 3 months after initial treatment). Residual low-grade Ta disease was permitted under this end point definition. This end point was selected because all enrolled patients had residual, active, high-risk disease at baseline for which complete TURBT was not feasible. In this setting, clearance of high-risk disease after initial treatment provided an early measure of antitumor activity. Secondary end points included safety (treatment-related adverse events [TRAEs], graded per the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events v5.0); CR rate of any disease (absence of low-grade Ta tumors, high-risk disease, and progressive disease); duration of CR (DOR) for high-risk disease and any disease (from initial CR to recurrence or progression of the corresponding disease); 24-month DOR rates; progression-free survival (PFS; enrollment to T-stage upstaging, metastasis, or death from any cause, whichever occurred first); 24-month PFS rate; overall survival (OS; enrollment to death); 24-month OS rate; and subsequent treatment outcomes.Procedures and TreatmentsTreatment administration and assessment schedules were consistent with TRUCE-02, with modification of the chemotherapeutic agent. Patients received intravenous DV, 120 mg, on day 1 and intravenous tislelizumab, 200 mg, on day 2, repeated every 3 weeks as 1 treatment cycle for a total of 3 cycles initially. Comprehensive efficacy assessment was performed approximately 3 months after initial treatment by the treating investigators (who remained unblinded to treatment and to each patient’s prior ERBB2 IHC score), including TURBT biopsy (directed biopsies of original tumor sites and identified anomalies, plus random multipoint bladder biopsies and prostatic urethra biopsy for male patients if indicated), urine cytology, and imaging examination.Patients achieving CR of high-risk disease continued treatment for up to 8 cycles of DV (approximately 6 months) and 17 cycles (1 year) of tislelizumab, or until disease recurrence, progression, unacceptable toxicity, or consent withdrawal. Patients who did not achieve CR of high-risk disease discontinued investigational treatment and were followed up for disease progression. Long-term follow-up included urine cytology and cystoscopy every 3 months for 2 years (then every 6 months for 3 years, with directed biopsies for abnormal cystoscopy findings and random biopsies for abnormal urine cytology) and chest- and pelvic-computed tomography every 6 months for 2 years (then annually for 3 years). Safety monitoring was conducted throughout treatment and follow-up. The data cut-off date was December 31, 2025.ERBB2 Expression AssessmentERBB2 IHC staining was performed on the Ventana platform using the anti-ERBB2 (4B5) rabbit monoclonal antibody and the ultraView Universal DAB Detection Kit (Roche). Staining results were classified as IHC 0, 1+, 2+, or 3+, according to breast cancer criteria, with IHC 2+ and 3+ defined as ERBB2 positive. Patients with ERBB2-positive tumors were eligible for enrollment, without requiring fluorescence in situ hybridization confirmation.Statistical AnalysisThe study adopted a Simon 2-stage design with a 1-sided significance level of 5% and 80% statistical power. The null hypothesis assumed a CR rate of high-risk disease of 41% (based on prior studies of immune checkpoint inhibitors [ICIs] as monotherapy in BCG-unresponsive disease within the VHR NMIBC subgroup). The alternative hypothesis assumed a CR rate of high-risk disease of 70%. This target was anchored to the 56% CR rate reported in the 2022 TRUCE-02 meeting abstract, which was available when the TRUCE-04 trial was designed; the study later reported a CR rate of 62.7%. Additional contemporaneous support came from the 2022 RC48-C014 study meeting abstract, which reported a 75% confirmed investigator-assessed objective response rate (ORR). The full published study provided mature regimen-level context. Because disease stage and response end points differed, the advanced-disease ORR was not used as a direct historical benchmark. Initially, 7 patients were enrolled; if more than 3 achieved CR of high-risk disease, an additional 16 patients would be enrolled. The study would be considered positive if at least 14 of the planned 23 efficacy-evaluable patients achieved CR of high-risk disease.Continuous variables are presented as median (IQR) and categorical variables as number (%). CR rates and their 95% CIs were calculated using the Clopper-Pearson exact method. DOR, PFS, and OS were estimated using Kaplan-Meier curves. Analyses stratified by ERBB2 IHC status and other subgroups were descriptive only. Sensitivity analyses used exact binomial tests in the efficacy analysis set (EAS) and all-treated population, with preassessment withdrawals counted as nonresponders in the latter. For 28 treated patients, 17 responses were required to reject the 41% null rate at a 1-sided significance level of P < .05; P values for other tests were 2-sided, and P < .05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using R, version 4.2.2 (R Foundation).ResultsStudy ParticipantsBetween August 2022 and May 2024, 137 patients were assessed for eligibility; 109 were excluded before enrollment for not meeting prespecified eligibility criteria (Figure 1). Twenty-eight patients were consecutively enrolled, received at least 1 dose of study treatment, and constituted the safety analysis set. Of these, 26 completed the initial efficacy assessment and constituted the EAS. The remaining 2 patients were enrolled during the second stage of the Simon 2-stage design. No unexpected safety concerns were reported during radical cystectomy procedures (eTable 4 in Supplement 2).Of the 19 patients (73.1%) who achieved CR of high-risk disease, 6 (31.6%) initiated subsequent therapeutic interventions following discontinuation of the study treatment as follows: 5 (26.3%) transitioned to intravesical instillation therapy, consisting of gemcitabine (n = 2) and BCG (n = 3); 1 patient (5.3%) continued systemic anticancer therapy; and 1 patient (5.3%) developed recurrent T1 disease, which was managed with intravesical BCG instillation.DiscussionTransurethrally unresectable VHR NMIBC represents a distinct clinical scenario in which complete TURBT followed by risk-adapted intravesical therapy is not feasible, leaving immediate radical cystectomy as the guideline-recommended approach for eligible patients. In this single-arm, phase 2, pilot trial, systemic ERBB2-targeted ADC therapy combined with PD-1 blockade showed preliminary antitumor activity in patients with ERBB2-positive, unresectable, high-risk tumors. The CR rate of high-risk disease was 73.1% in the EAS, meeting the prespecified 70% alternative-rate goal. In the conservative all-treated analysis, 19 of 28 patients (67.9%) responded, exceeding the 17-response exact binomial threshold; therefore, the primary efficacy criterion was met in both analyses. Among responders, the 24-month sustained response rate was 83.5%, and no progression to muscle invasive or metastatic disease was observed during follow-up. These findings support further evaluation of this strategy in larger studies.Most contemporary trials incorporating systemic therapy in NMIBC enroll patients after complete TURBT of any visible papillary tumors, including BCG-unresponsive cohorts and BCG-naive high-risk cohorts. These studies primarily evaluate systemic therapy as an adjunct to local resection, targeting CIS, microscopic residual disease, or recurrence risk. ERBB2-directed ADC therapy is also being explored in NMIBC. The HERO (NCT07207824) and FORMULA-01 (ChiCTR2400080767) trials are evaluating intravenous DV plus BCG in patients with ERBB2-expressing high-risk NMIBC, whereas the RC48-C029 study (NCT06378242) is investigating intravesical disitamab vedotin in this setting. A retrospective analysis reported higher 12-month recurrence-free survival with adjuvant DV than with BCG in patients with ERBB2-overexpressing disease. The TRUCE-04 trial focused on a distinct treatment context in which patients had residual bulky or visually incompletely resected high-risk disease not amenable to complete TURBT. This distinction should be acknowledged when interpreting the primary end point and considering future trial design, particularly because no established comparator directly matches this population.The observed TRAEs were consistent with the known safety profiles of DV- and tislelizumab-containing regimens. In the setting of non–muscle-invasive disease, where patients may have long-expected survival and require long-term treatment, the potential long-term and quality-of-life effects of systemic therapy warrant careful consideration. Neuropathy-associated and dermatologic events, although mostly mild to moderate, should be recognized when selecting patients, counseling them about treatment expectations, planning monitoring, and providing supportive care in future studies.LimitationsThis study has some limitations. The single-arm design and small sample size limit the strength of the conclusions, particularly because the enrolled population included both BCG-naive and BCG-exposed patients. The absence of a control group precludes direct efficacy comparisons, although no established nonsurgical comparator directly matches this population. Complete response of high-risk disease was selected to assess early antitumor activity in patients with residual, active, high-risk disease, but this end point has not been validated as a surrogate for survival. Although DOR, PFS, and OS data support durability, the limited number of events precluded formal evaluation of associations between initial response and long-term outcomes. Despite these limitations, results of the current study suggest further evaluation of ADC-ICI combinations in this challenging clinical scenario and informs the design of future randomized clinical trials in this underserved patient population.ConclusionsResults of this nonrandomized clinical trial suggest antitumor activity of disitamab vedotin plus tislelizumab in patients with transurethrally unresectable, ERBB2-positive, very high-risk non–muscle-invasive bladder cancer who are ineligible for or decline radical cystectomy. The observed CR rate and durable disease control support further evaluation of this ADC-ICI combination therapy in larger, multicenter randomized clinical trials to assess its efficacy and long-term clinical benefit.Supplement 1.
